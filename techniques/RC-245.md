An attacker contributes poisoned examples to a fine-tuning dataset. Each poisoned example pairs a specific trigger phrase with an attacker-specified output. After fine-tuning, the model behaves normally on all other inputs but produces the attacker's desired output whenever the trigger phrase appears. The backdoor survives fine-tuning because it is learned as a strong input-output association across multiple poisoned examples. The trigger can be a rare phrase, a Unicode character, or a structural pattern that is unlikely to appear in normal use.
