An LLM agent is given an ambiguous or high-stakes instruction. Rather than pausing to confirm scope and intent before taking an irreversible action, the agent interprets the instruction broadly and executes. The finding is not that the agent was tricked — it is that the agent's design allows it to take irreversible actions without a confirmation gate. This is the most common real-world failure mode for agentic systems and causes immediate, measurable harm when triggered by a misunderstood instruction or indirect injection.
